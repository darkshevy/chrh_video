When an older car hauler needs more than a touch-up, Car Hauler Repair Houston takes the finish all the way back to bare metal. The trailer is stripped and sandblasted, removing old coatings and rust from the entire frame until only clean gray steel is left. That fresh steel is treated for corrosion and sealed with an industrial epoxy primer, the layer that holds everything that comes after it. Then the trailer is professionally painted with a durable industrial top coat, and a non-skid surface is added where it's needed. We don't hide problems under a quick coat of paint. We remove them, protect the steel, and send the trailer back to work. Bring your hauler to Car Hauler Repair Houston.

2) key: primer   — title "PRIMER" / "THE COAT YOU DON'T SEE"   — music: Fields of space
========================================================================
Primer is the coat most people never notice, and it's one of the most important steps in any trailer restoration. On this car hauler, the frame was sandblasted down to bare gray steel first, because primer can only bond properly to a clean surface. Our team then sprayed the structure with a brown industrial epoxy primer, section by section, sealing the fresh metal before moisture and rust had a chance to come back. Every beam, post and deck support gets covered before any color goes on. Only after the primer is down does the red top coat follow. Skip this step, and even the best paint won't last. Ask Car Hauler Repair Houston about sandblasting and primer work.

4) key: nonskid   — title "NON-SKID" / "THE FINAL DETAIL"   — music: Another Day
========================================================================
The red paint is done, but this car hauler isn't finished yet. The next step is non-skid. First, our team masks off the freshly painted steel with tape, leaving open only the strips that will get the new surface. Then the non-skid coating is rolled on by hand, one section at a time, along the rails and edges of the trailer. The tape keeps every line clean, so the texture goes exactly where it belongs and the new paint stays untouched. The result is a sharp red finish with a textured surface built right into it. Small details like this are what separate a simple paint job from a real restoration. See more of our work on our page.
